## What Free-Flow is

**Free-Flow** means driving without stopping at a booth: roadside **cameras read the licence plate**, the operator posts a debt after the trip, and EuroGoPass (when Free-Flow is in the cart) **saves the card at checkout**, then **charges the official amount later** plus a clear **10% processing fee**. Checkout shows an **estimate**; the **final charge follows real camera data**, not the estimate alone.

This is **not** an e-vignette (time-based national pass). France, Norway, and Sweden do **not** sell a classic EuroGoPass e-vignette for the whole motorway network.

## France — Free-Flow vs gated péage

1. **Gated péage** — ticket / pay at barriers by distance. **Not** the Free-Flow product. Barrier tolls are generally paid on the road.
2. **Free-Flow (Flux Libre)** — cameras only. EuroGoPass can include these sections as **France Free-Flow**.

Current camera corridors include examples such as A13 Paris–Caen (SAPN), A14 Montesson / Chambourcy (SAPN), A79 Montmarault–Digoin (ALIAE), A4 Boulay-Moselle near Metz (Sanef) — not the entire A4.

Say “selected Free-Flow camera sections”. Do **not** claim EuroGoPass covers every French motorway or replaces all péage payments.

### France customer journey

1. Plan route → Free-Flow appears if sections are on the route (or add manually).
2. Pay vignettes/bridges due now; Free-Flow is **authorized for later** (card saved).
3. Drive; cameras record the plate.
4. After travel, EuroGoPass charges **official debt + 10%**.
5. If the card charge fails, the driver must settle France themselves (copy often cites **72 hours**). EuroGoPass is **not liable for fines** if the saved card cannot be charged.

## Norway — Free-Flow

- **No national highway vignette** for passenger cars.
- Many motorways, tunnels, and city approaches use electronic free-flow (often discussed as AutoPASS). Foreign plates are typically billed via brokers such as **EPASS24** — that portal name is **ops language**; the EuroGoPass **product** is still **Free-Flow**.
- Checkout shows an **estimate**; **actual charge** follows scraped portal debt after travel.
- Nordic Free-Flow uses a **monthly debt window**: for travel in calendar month **M**, debt is typically handled from about **day 15 of month M+1** through **end of month M+2**. Do **not** write “you are charged the next day” for Norway/Sweden.

## Sweden — Free-Flow + congestion tax

- **No national highway vignette**. Most long-distance motorways are **free**.
- **Congestion tax** applies in **Stockholm** and **Gothenburg** (time-of-day rules; often free on weekends, public holidays, and much of July — exact calendar is official, do not invent exceptions).
- Some **bridges / infrastructure** also charge electronically.
- **Sweden Free-Flow** covers these camera / electronic charges in the same deferred model as Norway.

## Øresund Bridge (not Free-Flow)

- Fixed link **Copenhagen (DK) ↔ Malmö (SE)**.
- Optional **bridge toll product** under Sweden (`se-oresund`) — **prepaid online ticket**, not camera Free-Flow.
- Prefer “check live price on EuroGoPass” over hardcoding. Walk-up rates are often higher than online tickets.
- Denmark motorways are otherwise **free** for typical cars under 3.5 t — Storebælt and Øresund are the famous paid bridges.
- Call it **Øresund Bridge** / bridge toll — **never** “Sweden Free-Flow” or “EPASS24”.
